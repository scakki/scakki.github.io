01 / Resilience under faultsSimulation
0%↗72%
BeforeWith SAC control

Walking success · two joint-lock faults · Unitree G1

Learning + control2026

Keep moving.
Even when a joint can’t.

Fault-tolerant locomotion for a 23-DoF humanoid. I developed SAC controllers and torque-limiting controls to study how a robot can keep making progress with locked joints.

Unitree G1SACMuJoCo
Inside the project +

Challenge. Joint locks change what movements are physically possible. A controller must adapt while managing actuator loads.

Results. Two-lock walking success increased from 0% to 72%. With a hip locked, torque limits reduced peak torque by 26%. A separate crawling policy achieved 92.5% survival with forward progress under three knee/ankle locks, with hips free.

RoLAC Lab · August–September 2026. These are simulation results under distinct fault conditions.

02 / Gait discoveryNASA-funded
WalkingRunningSkipping

One gait-agnostic reward · conceptual illustration

Locomotion + biomechanics2025 — ongoing

One reward.
Three ways to move.

I built a compliant planar biped and a learning pipeline where walking, aerial running, and skipping emerge without gait labels or prescribed contact schedules.

SACGymnasiumEarth / lunar gravity
Inside the project +

Approach. A 9-DoF model in MuJoCo learns with a single gait-agnostic reward, without reference trajectories.

Evaluation. Frozen controllers achieved 100% fall-free trials across the three gaits, with 0.07–0.26 m/s velocity-tracking RMSE and 0.44–0.84 mechanical cost of transport.

Next. An 18-muscle-actuator model for studying efficient movement in Earth and lunar gravity.

RoLAC Lab · June 2025–present · NASA-funded. Results refer to evaluated simulation trials.

03 / Perception to actionPhysical Go1

Sense → reason → plan → move · conceptual illustration

Autonomy + robotic security2025

From a language goal
to a robot’s next step.

I integrated VLM–RL navigation with LiDAR, RealSense/YOLO perception, and A* planning on a physical Unitree Go1, then evaluated susceptibility to prompt injection.

VLM / RLROSRealSense
Inside the project +

System. Perception and mapping inform high-level goal selection; path planning connects those goals to learned locomotion.

Evaluation. Navigation experiments measured task completion and completion time across randomized physical-robot trials. Security experiments tested redirection without changing VLM weights or training data.

Why it matters. A robot can execute a route successfully while following the wrong high-level instruction. Evaluating autonomy means examining the whole perception-to-action chain.

RoLAC Lab · January–March 2025 · Physical robot experiments.

04 / Comparing controllersNSF-funded
MPC2.993
PPO1.768

Mechanical cost of transport · lower is better

Benchmarking + control2023 — 2024

Same robot. Same test.
Different controllers.

I built a matched MuJoCo benchmark for PPO and predictive-sampling MPC. PPO achieved 41% lower mechanical cost of transport and recovered from disturbances faster.

PPOMPCIEEE Access
Inside the project +

Method. A 12-DoF Go1 at 0.5 m/s, with identical terrain and disturbance conditions for both controllers.

Results. Mechanical cost of transport was 1.768 for PPO versus 2.993 for MPC. Disturbance recovery was 0.25–0.33 s faster for PPO.

Michigan Technological University · August 2023–March 2024 · NSF-funded. Published in 2025; findings apply to the benchmark conditions.

I’m Shivayogi Akki, a Ph.D. candidate exploring how learning and control can make movement more capable and efficient.

At Binghamton University’s Robotics, Locomotion, and Applied Control Lab, my work spans humanoid fault tolerance, human-inspired gait discovery, and autonomous quadrupeds. My current research asks how locomotion changes across Earth and lunar gravity.

My path runs from mechanical engineering to mechatronics and now electrical and computer engineering. That perspective keeps me interested in the whole system: the learning algorithm, the controller, and the machine that has to move.
